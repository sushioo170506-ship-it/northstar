# Competitive Positioning

A model evaluated in a vacuum can't support a choice. Place it among its real
alternatives and articulate the trade-offs.

## Inputs
- Outputs of all Phase C pillars (capability, cost/TCO, ops, risk); the weighted
  criteria from `decision-framing`.

## Steps
1. **Identify the realistic alternative set** — rival models/tiers, the
   incumbent, open-weight options, and the "do nothing / status quo" baseline.
2. **Compare on the same axes** used throughout (the weighted criteria), using
   the same evidence standard — don't compare your deep analysis of one model to
   marketing blurbs of another; note asymmetries in evidence quality.
3. **Locate the trade-off frontier.** Where does the model dominate, where is it
   dominated, and where is it a trade (e.g., best capability but highest cost and
   highest autonomy risk)? Identify which options are Pareto-inferior.
4. **Write conditional guidance:** "Choose this model when <conditions/priorities>;
   choose <alternative> when <conditions>." Tie conditions to the workload profile.
5. **Note dynamics:** release cadence and roadmap can flip positioning soon —
   coordinate with temporal/watch-list notes.

## Output (feeds `comparison-synthesis` and `research-report-authoring`)
- Positioning analysis on the trade-off frontier.
- "When to choose which" decision guidance.
- Evidence-asymmetry caveats.

## Quality checks
- Alternatives (incl. status quo) are compared on the same weighted axes.
- Guidance is conditional and tied to the user's priorities, not a blanket "best".
- Differences in evidence quality across candidates are disclosed.
